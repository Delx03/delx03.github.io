Meconopsis, Blue Poppy

Himalayan Blue Poppy

Description

The Himalayan blue poppy (Meconopsis
betonicifolia) is a perennial plant of
the genus Meconopsis mainly found in the
mountainous regions of the Himalayas in Nepal
and Tibet. It is known for its striking blue
flowers and is typically found at
altitudes between 2,000 and 5,000 meters.
It blooms from June to July and usually
lives for two to three years, flowering in
the second or third year.

Appearance

Himalayan blue poppies usually grow to about 1 to 2 meters in height. The flowers measure
6 to 12 centimeters in diameter, and can be sky-blue or deep blue. The flowers are shaped like
a bowl or cup and grow on a stem with fine, bristly hairs and pale green leaves.

Symbolism

The Himalayan blue poppy is often associated with the beauty and resilience of the Himalayan landscape.
The way it flourishes in such harsh and high-altitude conditions symbolizes hope and perseverance in the
face of adversity. In the local culture, it is also seen as a symbol of inner peace and tranquility,
inspiring artists, gardeners, and poets alike.
